From house deed to real estate share token to lending yield

A $500,000 example home (illustrative) is held by an SPV. The SPV's equity is divided into 500,000 shares, each represented 1:1 by a Stellar real estate share token (asset code HOUSE1, a made-up code). The property's value is recorded on Stellar testnet, and up to 50% of it is put to work in Blend lending pools. The yield comes from lending interest, not from rent and not from the house itself.

  • Off-chain: title + appraisal, SPV legal wrapper, mortgage servicer
  • On Stellar testnet: share token, Soroban vault, Blend supply, Reflector (SEP-40) pricing
  • Non-custodial wallets: Freighter · LOBSTR · xBull · Scopuly (the app never holds keys)

HOW THE SHARE TOKEN WORKS ON STELLAR

Share register & legal wrapper (off-chain)

The SPV's operating agreement says its equity is divided into 500,000 shares and that each share is represented 1:1 by the HOUSE1 asset. The share register ties each verified holder to a wallet address. Which record legally governs (the ledger or an off-chain register) depends on the jurisdiction and the documents. Title and appraisal remain off-chain.

Issuer & distribution accounts

An issuer account creates the asset; a separate distribution account holds the minted supply and sends it to holders. Holders opt in with a trustline (change_trust). The issuer sets AUTH_REQUIRED (approve each holder first), AUTH_REVOCABLE (freeze/limit) and AUTH_CLAWBACK_ENABLED (recover tokens for legal or compliance reasons; it requires revocable). Because the issuer must keep these powers, it cannot be locked, so it needs multisig and governance.

Compliance: SEP-8, SEP-24 (and SEP-10/12)

SEP-8 regulated assets: an approval server checks and signs each transfer (holder limits, lock-ups, sanctions screening). SEP-24 gives an interactive web flow where an anchor/issuer can run KYC and funding; SEP-12 is the KYC data API and SEP-10 proves wallet ownership. The asset's stellar.toml (SEP-1) advertises these.

Soroban vault, Blend & Reflector

A Soroban vault contract tracks the recorded property value and the share supply, and uses the asset's SEP-41-style token interface (the Stellar Asset Contract). It supplies up to 50% of the value to a Blend pool (a submit call with a supply request) to earn borrower interest, and reads Reflector oracle prices (SEP-40 lastprice) to value positions, including XLM conversion.

Where the yield comes from, and the risks

Lending interest. Borrowers in the Blend pool pay interest; lenders earn a variable share. Not rent. Risks: smart-contract bugs, oracle errors, borrower liquidation shortfalls / bad debt, liquidity limits on withdrawal, rate swings, regulatory change, and the fact that a house cannot itself be lent: real funds would need to back whatever is supplied.
